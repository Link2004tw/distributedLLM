Rabbit Fun Facts

1. Rabbits are not rodents but belong to their own family called Lagomorpha, making them related to hares and pikas.

2. A rabbit's ears can rotate nearly 180 degrees, allowing them to pinpoint exactly where sounds come from while also helping regulate body temperature.

3. Rabbits have nearly 360-degree vision thanks to eyes positioned on the sides of their heads, though they have a small blind spot directly in front of their nose.

4. A rabbit's teeth never stop growing - they can grow up to 5 inches per year and must be worn down through constant chewing on hay, grass, and chew toys.

5. Rabbits can live 10 to 12 years or more with proper care, making them a long-term commitment like a dog or cat.

6. Rabbits cannot vomit, so it is crucially important to keep harmful objects and foods away from them as nothing can come back up.

7. Like cats, baby rabbits are called kittens, and they can purr when happy by gently chattering their teeth together.

8. Rabbits are social animals and need companionship - a lonely rabbit can become depressed and stop eating, which can be fatal.

9. Rabbits do two types of poop: regular dry pellets and nutrient-rich cecotropes that they actually eat to obtain essential vitamins and nutrients.

10. Carrots are actually not good for rabbits - they contain too much sugar and not enough fiber, leading to digestive problems.

11. Rabbits can be litter trained just like cats, using a corner of their enclosure or a designated rabbit litter box.

12. When a rabbit is truly happy, it performs a "binky" - jumping into the air and twisting its head and body in opposite directions.

13. Rabbits can jump up to 3 feet high and run at speeds up to 35 miles per hour in short bursts.

14. Rabbits are crepuscular, meaning they are most active at dawn and dusk, just like their wild ancestors.

15. A wild rabbit named Flopsy was the oldest on record, living over 18 years, though the average wild rabbit lives only about 2 years.

16. Rabbits groom themselves as meticulously as cats, spending a significant portion of their day cleaning their fur.

17. Rabbits can sleep with their eyes open as a defense mechanism, though they prefer to sleep with eyes closed in safe environments.

18. Rabbits have a very delicate skeleton - nearly 20 percent of their body weight is just their muscles, and they can be seriously injured by mishandling.

19. A rabbit can thump its hind feet to warn others of danger - this behavior can produce vibrations that travel through the ground.

20. Rabbits eat their own cecotropes, which contain essential nutrients and vitamin B that their digestive system cannot otherwise absorb.

21. A male rabbit is called a buck, a female is a doe, and a baby is called a kit or kitten.

22. Rabbit tails are called "scuts," and the cotton-tail is one of the most recognizable features in popular culture.

23. Rabbits love to dig and chew, which are natural behaviors they must express - providing toys and boxes helps satisfy these instincts.

24. Some rabbits can learn their own names and come when called, and they can recognize their owner's face, smell, and voice.

25. Rabbits are considered herbivores whose diet should be about 80 percent hay, 15 percent fresh vegetables, and 5 percent quality pellet food.

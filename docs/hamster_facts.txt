Hamster Fun Facts

1. Hamsters are crepuscular, meaning they are most active during dawn and dusk. In the wild, they sleep during the day and forage for food at night.

2. Hamsters have expandable cheek pouches that can stretch from their cheeks all the way to their shoulders, allowing them to carry food equal to half their body weight.

3. The five hamster species most commonly kept as pets are: Roborovski, Syrian, Campbell's Dwarf, Chinese, and Winter White Russian Dwarf.

4. Hamsters are omnivores. In the wild, they eat a mix of plants, seeds, insects, and small vertebrates. Pet hamsters thrive onpelleted diets with occasional fresh vegetables and protein.

5. A hamster's incisor teeth never stop growing. They need plenty of chew toys and safe materials to gnaw on to keep their teeth worn down.

6. Syrian hamsters are strictly solitary and must live alone. They will fight and potentially kill cage mates if housed together, even if raised together from a young age.

7. Dwarf hamsters can be social and may live in pairs or small same-sex groups if introduced at a young age and given enough space. However, they should still be monitored for fighting.

8. The average lifespan of a pet hamster is 2 to 3 years, though some can live up to 4 years with ideal care. Syrian hamsters tend to live longer than dwarf varieties.

9. The smallest hamster species is the Roborovski Dwarf Hamster, measuring just 2 to 4 inches long. The largest is the European Hamster, which can grow up to 13 inches.

10. Hamsters are born blind and deaf. Their eyes don't open until they are about two weeks old, and they begin to hear around the same time.

11. A single female hamster can give birth to up to 24 babies at a time, though the average litter size is around 7 pups. They can reproduce as early as 6 weeks old.

12. Hamsters have poor eyesight and rely on their whiskers to navigate. They can only see a few inches in front of them and are near-sighted.

13. In the wild, hamsters can run 5 to 8 kilometers each night while foraging for food. This is why they need large enclosures and exercise wheels.

14. The ideal temperature for hamsters is between 18°C to 24°C (65°F to 75°F). Temperatures above 30°C can be fatal, and they are prone to heat stroke.

15. Hamsters have a specialized scent gland on their flanks called a ventral gland. They use it to mark their territory by rubbing against surfaces.

16. Hamsters are excellent climbers but poor climbers. They prefer horizontal floor space over vertical height and need enclosures with deep bedding to burrow.

17. The most common health problem in hamsters is "wet tail," a bacterial diarrhea that can be fatal within 24 to 48 hours if not treated by a veterinarian.

18. Hamsters have 42 teeth, just like dogs. They are born with all their teeth and their incisors never stop growing throughout their lives.

19. Female hamsters have a territorial behavior called "scatter hoarding" where they hide food throughout their cage rather than storing it in one place.

20. Hamsters can enter a state of torpor or light hibernation if the temperature drops too low, though this can be dangerous and should be avoided.

21. The first golden hamsters were discovered in Syria in 1930 and were brought to the United States in 1938. All modern pet Syrian hamsters descend from this original breeding group.

22. Hamsters have a third eyelid called a nictitating membrane that helps protect and lubricate their eyes, especially when they are burrowing.

23. An exercise wheel is essential for a hamster's mental and physical health. Female hamsters have been known to run up to 10 kilometers in a single night on their wheel.

24. Hamsters can be pregnant for just 15 to 18 days, one of the shortest gestation periods of any mammal. This is why their babies are so underdeveloped at birth.

25. The record for the most babies born to a single hamster in one litter is 24. However, the mother usually cannot nurse all of them, so careful breeders must assist with hand-feeding.
